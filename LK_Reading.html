Integral-supported anchor papers

These labels record Integral’s current technical view; they do not imply universal scientific consensus.

DeForest et al. (2012)

Fish-egg species sensitivity distribution and tissue guideline framework

Integral position: supported as a key effects-threshold framework.
Central question
How should chronic selenium effects data across fish species be organized into an egg/ovary benchmark?
Important boundary
The paper develops a guideline framework; applying it to Montana still requires an explicit regulatory and site-applicability analysis.

Evidence-context note: Thomas & Janz (2015) is retained as an important contrasting sensitivity study to review alongside this framework.

Read alongside
Contrasting contextThomas & Janz (2015) adds a zebrafish developmental-toxicity result relevant to sensitivity assumptions.
Related evidenceBrix et al. (2025) adds species-specific early-life-stage evidence for mountain whitefish.
FrameworkEPA (2016/2021) provides the federal tissue-based criterion context.

Cochrane et al. (2025)

Bull trout population dynamics in Lake Koocanusa

Integral position: supported as evidence that prey availability, angling restrictions, density dependence, and hydrology are important population drivers.
Central question
Which measured ecological and management factors help explain bull trout population dynamics?
Important boundary
Do not convert a multi-driver finding into a claim that selenium has no effect unless the study directly estimated that effect.

Evidence-context note: This recent paper should be compared directly with local tissue and ecological evidence rather than judged by citation counts.

Read alongside
Ecological alignmentBalmer et al. (2025) also identifies hydrology, prey, and habitat as plausible drivers of observed wildlife patterns.
Different endpointMolbert et al. (2026) supplies local fish-tissue and trophodynamic evidence rather than a population-driver model.

Balmer et al. (2025)

Tree swallow demographics and egg metal(loid)s in the Montana Kootenai River basin

Integral position: supported as local avian and aquatic-terrestrial food-web evidence.
What the paper found
Across 2022–2023, egg selenium was below levels identified to cause avian reproductive effects, and hatchability was high. Selenium was higher in 2023 than 2022, but the authors caution that two years and limited northern sample sizes are insufficient for a long-term trend conclusion.
Important boundary
This is a tree-swallow egg and habitat study, not a fish-tissue criterion test. The paper recommends longer-term monitoring and additional diet/foraging information.

Full-text locator: Abstract; Results pp. 9–10; Discussion pp. 12–15; Conclusions and funding p. 16.

Read alongside
Ecological alignmentCochrane et al. (2025) reinforces the need to consider prey, management, and hydrology when interpreting biological patterns.
Cited source contextBalmer cites Storb (2023) and Foster (2024) for loading and transport context; that citation does not independently resolve Integral’s concerns about those papers’ interpretations.

Integral-disputed anchor sources

“Disputed” records Integral’s technical interpretation to investigate; it is not a retraction, misconduct finding, or universal consensus judgment.

Montana DEQ (2020)

Technical support document for the site-specific water-column standard

Integral concern: disagreement with how the model was interpreted and used to derive the scientific basis for the standard.
Central review question
Which modeled inputs, parameter choices, endpoints, and conservatism decisions drove the water-column value, and how should later measured tissue data test those choices?
Keep separate
The agency record is the primary source for the adopted derivation even where Integral disputes its scientific interpretation.

Evidence-context note: Preserve the agency document as the authoritative record of the adopted derivation while separately documenting Integral’s technical objections.

Read alongside
Underlying modelPresser & Naftz (2020) documents the modeling pathway used in the derivation.
Method comparisonReash et al. (2021) shows that different site-specific methods can produce different water-column results in another system.
Measured evidenceMolbert et al. (2026) adds later site-specific tissue data that should be compared with model outputs on matched species, tissues, stations, and periods.

Presser & Naftz (2020)

USGS ecosystem-scale modeling framework for Lake Koocanusa

Integral concern: the same general concern as for Montana DEQ—disagreement with the model’s interpretation and its use in deriving the standard.
Central review question
Do the selected partitioning, food-web, tissue-conversion, station, depth, and temporal inputs appropriately represent the reservoir and intended endpoint?
Keep separate
A modeled water-to-tissue relationship is not the same evidence type as measured tissue observations.

Evidence-context note: Evaluate the model against its stated assumptions and matched site-specific observations rather than citation frequency.

Read alongside
Historical methodAdams et al. (2000) provides earlier lotic/lentic site-specific methodology, with predominantly warmwater fish data and no peer review.
Threshold frameworkDeForest et al. (2012) addresses the fish-egg effects threshold rather than reservoir transport-model calibration.
Tissue interpretationDetering et al. (2025) can inform whether cross-tissue comparisons are appropriately matched.

Storb et al. (2023)
