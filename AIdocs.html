Three questions the references do not fully answer

Dead end

What makes a tool “protected”?

1Employee Handbook, §10.3.3, p. 60Staff may not use generative AI that does not comply with the AUP.
2AUP, p. 2Describes protected and non-protected tools by example only. No criteria are given.
3No definition reachedA reader cannot tell whether a particular tool is protected.

The definition is in footnote 1 of the AI QM Policy, which points back to the AUP.

Result

Ends at examples. No document points to the criteria.

Fix at the source

Put the definition in the AUP and list designated tools.

General rules only

What does QMP v.3 require for AI-assisted work?

1AUP, p. 2AI output must be verified consistent with the QMP.
2AI QM Policy, p. 3Non-material records must be sufficient for QA/QC review and audit, consistent with the QMP.
3QMP v.3, §3.6 and §6QA reviews must be adequately documented, review records kept in the project file, and record procedures set in the project work plan. AI is not mentioned.

The retention policy (2021) does not mention AI either.

Result

The general rules apply to AI work by implication. Nothing says what is adequate for AI records.

Fix at the source

QMP: one sentence that its review and records rules apply to AI work. AI QM Policy: what is adequate for non-material use.

Loop

Which disclosure policy governs a non-testifying expert on a litigation project?

1Non-lit disclosure, p. 1Litigation projects must follow the separate litigation policy.
2Litigation disclosure, p. 1For an exclusively non-testifying expert, the non-litigation policy remains in effect.
Points back to step 1

The litigation policy adds some voluntary-disclosure guidance for this role.

Result

Each sends the reader to the other. Neither says which governs.

Fix at the source

Add one sentence to both stating which governs.

ConflictIs ChatGPT a protected tool?Six documents, no single answer.

What each document says

  • Employee Handbook§10.3.3, p. 60Named as a riskNames ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot, the recommended tool, as sources of confidentiality risk. Staff may not use generative AI that does not comply with the AUP.
  • AUPp. 2Depends on settingsChatGPT with data controls is protected. ChatGPT with limited or no data controls is non-protected. Examples only, no criteria.
  • AUPp. 5Settings may not countTreat information in non-protected tools as public, regardless of the setting selected.
  • AI QM Policyfootnote 1, p. 1Depends on controlsRepeats the two examples. Protected means data controls and privacy safeguards, a definition it attributes to the AUP.
  • Reimbursement pageTier 2Reimbursable after approvalNames ChatGPT as a tool staff can be reimbursed for after AI Council pre-approval. Does not say whether it is protected.
  • Disclosure policiesExhibit 2 (non-lit p. 8, litigation p. 7)Used on client workSample disclosure statements name ChatGPT-4.5 and GPT-4o as tools used on reports and client communications.

Suggested next step

Put the definition in the AUP, list designated protected tools, say whether consumer subscriptions qualify, and reconcile the setting line.

ConflictWho do I ask about an AI tool?Contacts differ across five documents.

What each document says

  • AUPp. 3Questions about protected or non-protected tools go to the director of strategic initiatives and innovation and the director of information technology.
  • Employee Handbook§10.3.3, p. 60Questions about the generative AI rule go to the Director of IT.
  • Reimbursement pageTier 2Questions about additional tools go to your Sponsor or the AI Council. The page also refers to an AI Committee.
  • Litigation disclosurep. 2If a client requests a different AI tool, staff need prior approval from the director of strategic initiatives and innovation and the director of contracts and risk services.

Suggested next step

DiffersHow does an AI tool get approved?Five routes, and none says how protected status is checked.

What each document says

  • Reimbursement pageTier 1Copilot is the company-supported starting point. The page points to setup instructions and describes no approval step.
  • Reimbursement pageTier 2AI Council reviewsOther tools, such as ChatGPT or Claude Pro, need a one-time pre-approval form with a business case, reviewed by the AI Council. Reimbursement is up to $25 a month. Staff must follow the AUP and are strongly encouraged to use two-factor authentication and an Integral email address.
  • Reimbursement pageTier 3Automation and development needs go to IT and the AI Committee.
  • Strategic Planp. 13Describes an enterprise AI subscription reimbursement program for staff adopting AI tools for client-facing work and internal operations.
  • AUPp. 3Questions about protected or non-protected tools go to the director of strategic initiatives and innovation and the director of information technology.
  • Litigation disclosurep. 2If a client requests a different AI tool, staff need prior approval from the director of strategic initiatives and innovation and the director of contracts and risk services.
  • QMP v.3§7.2Nonstandard software needs a written business justification approved by the PIC or project manager and by the operations director or vice president of operations. IT verifies the need for high-risk or high-cost items. AI is not mentioned.

AI Council and IT: state one approval route for AI tools, say how protected status is checked before a tool is reimbursed, and align the QMP software justification (7.2) with it.

DiffersWhat counts as material AI use?Same test, different outcomes.

What each document says

  • Non-lit disclosurep. 4, Exhibit 1Material means the AI output substantively shapes conclusions, recommendations or opinions. Voluntary disclosure may be considered.
  • Litigation disclosurep. 1, Exhibit 1Same test, applied to expert opinions. Disclosure to the client is recommended or required, and an added question asks whether AI is a key step in the expert’s reasoning.
  • AI QM Policypp. 2–3Uses materiality to set the level of documentation but points only to the non-litigation policy for its meaning.

Suggested next step

Have the AI QM Policy cite both disclosure policies.

DiffersWhat must be documented, and where does it go?Same five items, different strength, and a different rule for litigation.

What each document says

  • AUPp. 2Says AI output must be verified consistent with QMP v.3.
  • AI QM Policyp. 3For material use, staff should keep prompts, outputs, review steps, modifications and the rationale. For non-material use, records must be sufficient for QA/QC review and audit, consistent with QMP v.3.
  • Litigation disclosurep. 2Staff must keep contemporaneous records of the same five items, mark and segregate them as privileged, and limit internal distribution. The records may be discoverable.
  • Non-lit disclosurep. 5Suggests considering a record of why AI use was or was not disclosed.
  • QMP v.3§3.6, §6Has no AI section. Its general rules cover documented QA review and project records, but say nothing on AI records.
  • Retention policypp. 1–2, 8Written in April 2021. Covers project documents in general but does not mention AI prompts, outputs or verification notes.

Both policies defer to QMP v.3. Its general QA and records rules apply, but neither it nor the retention policy mentions AI records.

Suggested next step

Say where AI records are kept and which rule applies on litigation projects. Update the 2021 retention policy to mention AI.

GapWho tracks outside AI rules, and what wins?Policies cannot be rewritten for every outside change, and no one is assigned to log them, here or abroad.

What each document says

  • AUPp. 3Says to follow legal and contractual obligations. Does not mention licensing boards or who tracks new guidance.
  • AI QM PolicyallDoes not mention outside requirements.
  • Non-lit disclosurepp. 1–4As of March 2026, no law, regulation or guidance required disclosure. Staff are to stay informed about client-specific laws, a list that does not include licensing boards. Voluntary disclosure needs written PIC approval, and minor use usually is not disclosed.
  • Litigation disclosurep. 3Testifying experts review local court rules before submitting AI-generated work.
  • Non-lit disclosurep. 3Names the EU AI Act among laws clients may be subject to. No other country is named, and no process is described for tracking foreign rules.
  • Retention policypp. 4–5Parábolas Consulting operates under Mexican law. Staff contact the director of contracts and risk services, who engages consultants and outside counsel case by case.
  • Strategic Planpp. 6, 10Explores international expansion, including the European Union, through acquisitions of non-U.S. firms.
  • NJ LSRP guidancepp. 1–2Recommends disclosureApplies existing board rules to AI: independent judgment, documented decision-making, verification of AI results, certification only after a good-faith effort. Recommends that LSRPs always disclose in writing when AI contributed to their documents.

Suggested next step

Contracts and risk group, with the library: keep a dated reference log of outside AI rules (state, federal, licensing board and non-U.S.), name an owner, and have the policies point to the log instead of listing rules. Say who decides when one is stricter than ours.

DiffersDo AI prompts and outputs have to be kept?AI policies say keep. The retention policy says drafts are normally not kept.

What each document says

  • AI QM Policyp. 3For material use, staff should keep prompts, AI-generated outputs, review steps, modifications and the rationale for using AI.
  • Litigation disclosurep. 2Staff must preserve versions of documents that show when and how AI tools were used.
  • Retention policyAttachment B, p. 8Draft and interim versions are replaced by later versions and are not retained unless they are the final work product or a necessary basis for it, unless a written agreement requires otherwise.
  • Retention policypp. 2–4A legal hold overrides any destruction schedule. Work products that may become the focus of litigation are preserved indefinitely, with an annual review. Otherwise the basic legal period is generally three years after completion, or longer if the contract says so.

Suggested next step

State that AI documentation counts as a necessary basis for final work products, and update the retention policy to mention AI records.
