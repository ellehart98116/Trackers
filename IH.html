🪨 Artificial Stone & Crystalline Silica

Regulatory, legislative, and enforcement developments on engineered/artificial stone silica. 2026 developments logged chronologically; standing reference cards below.

📅 2026 Developments Most recent first · Updated from InsideOSHA monthly scan
Aug 11, 2026
MSHA poised to propose changes to Biden-era silica rule after OMB review
MSHA is poised to publish an NPRM after OMB completed pre-publication review Aug. 10. The proposed rule would "clarify and modify" provisions of the 2024 silica rule — covering exposure monitoring, methods of compliance, respiratory protection, and medical surveillance — while stating changes would be "cost-neutral or cost-savings" without reducing miner protections. The 8th Circuit stay of compliance deadlines remains in effect; MSHA has been under pressure from industry (who want relaxation) and labor unions (who oppose any rollback). A legal challenge from miner advocacy groups is anticipated if any weakening of the 50 μg/m³ PEL occurs. OMB held 14 stakeholder meetings before completing review.
Unified Agenda entry → FR — stay notification (Apr 6) → 📰 InsideOSHA Aug 11 (subscription — request article)
May 26, 2026
Cal/OSHA Standards Board grants petition to ban artificial stone fabrication
In a unanimous vote on May 21, the Cal/OSHA Standards Board granted WOEMA Petition No. 609, directing Cal/OSHA to prepare an emergency rulemaking finding to prohibit fabrication and installation of artificial stone products containing more than 1% crystalline silica. The board also directed Cal/OSHA to convene scientific and technical advisory committees. As of May 2026, CDPH has confirmed 562 cases of silicosis and 31 deaths linked to engineered stone in California (median age at diagnosis: 46). Experts note legal questions about whether the standards board has authority to ban a currently legal industry without legislative backing — lawsuits expected if a ban is adopted. Industry favors certification (ISFA proposal) over a ban. Emergency rulemaking process will take months; additional board vote required before any prohibition takes effect.
Cal DIR press release (official) → EHS Leaders summary → 📰 InsideOSHA May 26 (subscription — request article)
Feb 25, 2026
Cal/OSHA board asks county DAs to prosecute shops over silica violations
Cal/OSHA's standards board formally requested district attorneys in seven California counties expand enforcement and bring criminal charges against engineered stone fabrication shop owners violating crystalline silica standards. Reflects frustration with civil enforcement pace as silicosis cases continue rising.
Cal/OSHA Silica program → 📰 InsideOSHA Feb 25 (subscription — request article)
Jan 21, 2026 · updated Jun 3, 2026
H.R. 5437 — House bill to ban silicosis lawsuits against stone manufacturers
Protection of Lawful Commerce in Stone Slab Products Act, introduced by Rep. Tom McClintock (R-CA). Would prohibit all civil actions against manufacturers and sellers of stone slabs for silica injury caused during third-party fabrication — and would retroactively dismiss ~500 pending lawsuits, the majority filed by California workers with silicosis. House Judiciary subcommittee hearing Jan. 14; committee passed 16–7 on June 3, 2026, along party lines. Now pending before full House. David Michaels (former OSHA head) testified in opposition, noting current OSHA PEL was set before engineered stone existed and that resin-coated high-silica slabs are uniquely dangerous. Democrats characterized the bill as manufacturer immunity at workers' expense.
H.R. 5437 bill text (Congress.gov) → Safety+Health Magazine → 📰 InsideOSHA Jan 21 (subscription — request article)
Jan 16, 2026
ISFA proposes nationwide certification program as alternative to ban
The International Surface Fabricators Association (ISFA) proposed a nationwide shop certification and licensing program as an industry counter to the ban push, arguing fabrication can be done safely with proper controls. Worker safety advocates criticized the plan as too slow — ISFA projected 2027 implementation, during which an estimated 62 additional workers would develop silicosis (with ~10% fatality rate). Cal/OSHA board chair questioned enforcement mechanisms; ISFA did not provide specifics. Australia's post-ban data presented at same meeting: no job losses, multiple low-silica substitutes available at similar price points.
ISFA → 📰 InsideOSHA Jan 16 (subscription — request article)
📋 Standing Reference
Federal OSHA — Crystalline Silica Standards (GI & Construction)
Final Rule · In Effect
29 CFR 1910.1053 (GI) · 29 CFR 1926.1153 (Construction) · Effective 2017
PEL 50 μg/m³ (8-hr TWA); action level 25 μg/m³. Engineering controls, respiratory protection, exposure assessment, medical surveillance, and hazard communication required. Engineered stone countertop fabrication falls under the General Industry standard. OSHA's National Emphasis Program on Silica (CPL 03-00-023, 2023) targets fabrication shops. Note: the OSHA PEL was set before the engineered stone industry existed — current research indicates resin-coated high-silica stone is more hazardous than the silica studied during the rulemaking.
OSHA Silica Standards →
California — SB 20 (STOP Act), Permanent Silica Rules & Ban Rulemaking
Active rulemaking · Ban petition granted May 2026
SB 20 (signed Oct 2025) · Cal/OSHA Permanent Silica Standard (adopted Feb 2025) · Petition No. 609 (granted May 2026)
California has the most active regulatory track. SB 20 (Silicosis Training, Outreach, and Prevention Act) classified silicosis as a serious illness and mandated data-sharing between Cal/OSHA and CDPH. Permanent silica standards adopted Feb 2025. Cal/OSHA's Silica Special Emphasis Program has issued 900+ citations and $1.9M in penalties; 95% of shops visited not in full compliance. May 2026: standards board unanimously directed Cal/OSHA to prepare emergency rulemaking to ban fabrication of stone with >1% crystalline silica. Legal authority to ban (vs. regulate) is contested.
Cal DIR — May 2026 press release →
MSHA — 2024 Silica Rule (Stayed) & Pending NPRM
Stayed · NPRM imminent as of Aug 2026
30 CFR Part 60 · Biden-era final rule (Apr 2024) · 8th Circuit stay (Apr 2025) · SMI v. MSHA
MSHA's 2024 rule set a 50 μg/m³ PEL for all mines — first-ever for coal, halved limit for metal/nonmetal. Stayed by the 8th Circuit Apr 2025 while industry litigation proceeds. MSHA announced it would pursue "limited rulemaking to reconsider" the rule; OMB completed review Aug 10, 2026. NPRM expected imminently. Stated goal: clarify/modify without reducing protections. Mine Act § 101(a)(9) prohibits MSHA from reducing existing protections — any weakening will face legal challenge from labor and miner health groups.
MSHA Silica Rule resources →
Australia — Engineered Stone Ban (July 1, 2024)
Ban in Effect · Key international comparator
Safe Work Australia Model WHS Regulations · All states and territories implementing
Australia became the first country to prohibit the supply, processing, and installation of engineered stone. Multiple low-silica substitutes (natural stone, solid surface, ultra-compact porcelain, crushed glass) are available at similar price points. Post-ban: no job losses reported; worker shortage in the sector. Screening program found ~1 in 4 fabrication workers (19.5%) had developed silicosis — suggesting US national case count may be in the tens of thousands. Cited as model by Cal/OSHA and WOEMA; contested by industry.
Safe Work Australia →

🏥 NIOSH Guidance & RELs 3 items

Recent NIOSH health hazard evaluations, criteria documents, and recommended exposure limits relevant to IH practice.

NIOSH — Criteria Document: Occupational Exposure to Silica
Criteria Doc · Current
NIOSH DHHS Publication No. 2002-129 · Updated guidance ongoing
NIOSH REL for respirable crystalline silica is 0.05 mg/m³ as a 10-hr TWA. NIOSH considers this a risk-based limit, not a zero-risk limit — residual cancer and silicosis risk persists at the REL. NIOSH has issued HHEs for multiple engineered stone fabrication shops (2019–2024) documenting exceedances well above the REL and acute silicosis cases.
NIOSH Silica Criteria Doc →
NIOSH — Current Intelligence Bulletins & HHEs (Stone Fabrication)
Ongoing · Multiple publications
NIOSH HHE Program · 2019–present
NIOSH has conducted health hazard evaluations at countertop fabrication facilities in California, Texas, Colorado, and other states. Findings consistently document silica exposures exceeding 10× the REL, progressive massive fibrosis in young workers, and inadequate engineering controls. CIB on occupational exposure to RCS during engineered stone processing under development.
NIOSH HHE Program →
NIOSH — Occupational Carcinogens: Updated REL Framework
In Development
NIOSH · Risk-based REL updates · Ongoing
NIOSH is updating its approach to RELs for carcinogens to include risk characterization at the REL (excess risk per 1,000 workers) per the 2023 NIOSH Strategic Plan. This affects a range of IH-relevant chemicals. Crystalline silica (IARC Group 1 carcinogen) is among the substances affected. Practitioners should monitor NIOSH for updated criteria documents.

🌐 International Standards & Developments 4 items

Major international OEL-setting bodies and regulatory developments outside the US.

EU — Chemical Agents Directive (OEL for RCS)
Binding OEL · In Effect
Directive 2017/164/EU · Amended by 2019/130/EU · 0.1 mg/m³ respirable RCS
EU binding OEL for respirable crystalline silica (quartz) is 0.1 mg/m³, higher than US NIOSH/OSHA limits. Social partner agreement (NEPSI) on RCS provides voluntary practical recommendations. Engineered stone not separately banned in EU but member states are monitoring Australia's experience. ECHA reviewing additional silica-related classifications.
EU CAD →
UK — HSE Silica & Engineered Stone Guidance
WEL · 0.1 mg/m³ RCS
EH40 Workplace Exposure Limits · HSE · Updated 2020
UK Workplace Exposure Limit for RCS is 0.1 mg/m³ (8-hr TWA). HSE has published specific guidance for stone fabrication (including engineered stone), noting the severe silicosis risk and requiring wet working and LEV. No ban proposed; enforcement-based approach. HSE consulted on potential WEL reductions post-Brexit.
UK EH40 →
ACGIH TLV — Crystalline Silica (Quartz), Respirable
TLV · 0.025 mg/m³
ACGIH 2024 TLV-TWA · A2 Suspected Human Carcinogen
ACGIH's TLV for respirable quartz is 0.025 mg/m³ — half the OSHA/NIOSH PEL/REL — with an A2 carcinogen designation. Tridymite TLV is half the quartz TLV. ACGIH reviews TLVs annually; silica classification has been stable but ACGIH-BEI documentation updated to reflect engineered stone evidence. No BEI established.
ACGIH Silica TLV Doc →
Spain — Engineered Stone Regulation (Significant Activity)
Watch · Regulatory action ongoing
RD 1432/2023 (Spain) · Ministry of Labour
Spain enacted Royal Decree 1432/2023 introducing strict controls on artificial stone processing, including mandatory wet cutting, LEV, respiratory protection, and medical surveillance. Spain was an early mover in Europe given documented silicosis cases in the countertop sector (particularly Andalucía). Implementation monitoring ongoing; other EU member states watching for potential model legislation.
BOE RD 1432/2023 →

ℹ️ About & Methodology

Purpose

This tracker is an Integral Library Services product providing a monthly digest of IH and occupational health regulatory developments. It covers OSHA rulemaking, OEL updates from ACGIH/NIOSH/AIHA, and topic-specific deep dives (such as artificial stone/silica) added as practice needs evolve.

How to use

  • Monthly: Add notable InsideOSHA highlights and new regulatory actions to the News tab. Log date, source, and a brief summary.
  • Each February: Check the ACGIH NIC list and update the OEL Watchlist with new TLV changes.
  • As-needed: Add new JOEH/AIHA Synergist papers to the Literature section. Update rule cards when OSHA publishes NPRMs, final rules, or enforcement guidance.
  • AI Digest: Use the "Run monthly digest" button on the News tab for an on-demand AI summary of recent developments.
  • Need a paywalled article? InsideOSHA and some journal content requires a subscription. Contact Ellen in Library Services at ehartley@integral-corp.com to request access to a specific article.

Adding new topic areas

This tracker is designed to grow. New topic sections (beyond artificial stone/silica) can be added as practice needs are identified — contact Library Services to request a new area of coverage.

What this tracker does NOT cover

  • General environmental (EPA, state DEQ) regulations unless directly IH-relevant
  • Individual enforcement case tracking (handled through InsideOSHA daily)
  • Clinical occupational medicine (medical surveillance interpretation, clinical management)

Submitting additions

To suggest additions, corrections, or new topic areas, contact Ellen in Library Services at ehartley@integral-corp.com. Include a source URL, date, and a brief note on relevance.
